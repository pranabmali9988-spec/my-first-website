FIELD NOTES & GROWING TIPS

A little knowledge
helps things grow.

Simple starting points for common crops. Local season, altitude, soil and water conditions can change what works best.

FRUITING VEGETABLE

Tomato

Environment Warm, sunny conditions with good airflow. Protect plants from waterlogging and very cold weather.

Growing notes Start with healthy seedlings, use support stakes and water near the soil consistently. Avoid wetting leaves late in the day.

Soil & nutrients Use well-drained soil enriched with mature compost. Choose a balanced fertiliser plan based on soil testing and local crop advice.

VINE VEGETABLE

Cucumber

Environment Warm weather, plenty of sunlight and a site with good drainage.

Growing notes Sow in warm soil, provide room for vines or a trellis, and keep moisture steady while fruits develop.

Soil & nutrients Add mature compost before planting. Avoid excess nitrogen, which can encourage leaves instead of fruit; follow local soil-test advice.

FIELD CROP

Maize

Environment A warm growing period, open sunlight and soil that drains well but retains useful moisture.

Growing notes Use quality seed suited to the local season, sow at recommended spacing and keep the field free of early weed competition.

Soil & nutrients Incorporate compost or well-rotted manure. Split nutrient applications and fertiliser rates should follow local recommendations and soil tests.

CEREAL CROP

Rice

Environment Warm conditions and a reliable water supply. The best variety and field method depend on local elevation and water availability.

Growing notes Use healthy seed, prepare a level field and manage water carefully. Keep weeds under control during early growth.

Soil & nutrients Add organic matter where suitable. Nutrient needs vary by field and variety, so use soil testing and agricultural technician guidance before applying fertiliser.

✳
Fertiliser note

Compost and well-rotted farmyard manure can help build soil organic matter. Chemical fertiliser type and amount should be chosen for the specific crop and field using a soil test or advice from a local agriculture technician. More fertiliser does not automatically mean a better harvest.

Learn more from Nepal's NARC Horticulture crop guidance, the National Center for Vegetable Development and the Department of Agriculture.
